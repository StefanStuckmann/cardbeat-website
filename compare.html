Plotting with index cards on iPhone and iPad: your options.

Index cards are the classic way to find the structure of a novel or screenplay. Here is how the main ways to use them on Apple devices compare – including when CardBeat is not the right pick.

Pick CardBeat if…

  • you think by handwriting and moving things around, and want that on iPhone or iPad without the pile of paper;
  • your story lives in acts, sequences and scenes, and you want to see all of them at once;
  • you plot in a writers' room and want a clean board on the TV or projector;
  • you write the actual pages elsewhere – Final Draft, Fade In, Highland, Scrivener, Word – and need a separate place for structure;
  • you don't want another subscription, and care that the app collects no data.

Pick Scrivener if…

you want your outline and your manuscript in one project. Scrivener's corkboard shows each section of your draft as a card, so reordering cards reorders the text. That tight link is its strength – and the reason it feels less free than a plain board when you are still finding the story.

Pick Apple Freeform if…

you need real-time collaboration or a general-purpose canvas for images, links and notes, and you don't mind building your own card system. It is free and already on your device. It isn't built around story structure, though, so acts, card styles and a writers'-room view are up to you.

Stick with paper if…

you love the wall. Nothing beats a corkboard you can stand in front of. The trade-offs: no undo, no backup, no color changes without rewriting, and the board stays in one room.

Try CardBeat

CardBeat is free with two boards, which is enough to plot a whole project before you decide. Download it on the App Store or read the FAQ.
